# The Position — PhD Stipendiat at OUS

## Institution
Oslo University Hospital (Oslo universitetssykehus, OUS)
Seksjon for klinisk psykoseforskning

## Project title
"The night and day of bipolar disorder"

## Core research question
How do circadian rhythm disturbances relate to:
- Lifestyle factors
- Mood variation
- Risk of new illness episodes
...in people who are early in the course of bipolar disorder?

## Study design
Intensive longitudinal design — repeated measurements over time within individuals.

## Data sources
1. Actigraphy — wrist-worn accelerometer data capturing sleep/wake cycles, activity patterns, rest-activity rhythms
2. App-based self-report — Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) / experience sampling capturing mood, energy, sleep quality, daily activities

## Statistical methods focus
Direction and strength of associations in longitudinal data. Relevant methods include:
- Cross-lagged panel models (CLPM) and random-intercept CLPM (RI-CLPM)
- Dynamic structural equation models (DSEM)
- Vector autoregression (VAR) — including idiographic (N=1) approaches
- Multilevel models for intensive longitudinal data
- Time-series analysis
- Idiographic methods (person-centered, within-person)

## Clinical anchoring
Bipolarteamet at Nydalen DPS — patients are recruited and followed through the clinical service.
This means the PhD student will need to navigate a clinical research environment.

## Research group
Kliniske symptommekanismer, led by Professor Trine Vik Lagerberg (OUS / University of Oslo).

## What this position likely demands
- Enthusiasm for working with complex longitudinal data
- Comfort with R (statistical computing)
- Interest in bridging clinical psychiatry with quantitative methods
- Ability to collaborate across clinical staff, statisticians, and academic supervisors
- Motivation grounded in genuine curiosity about circadian biology and mental health
